Microplotter

Proposed

The project folder holds a project proposal — objectives, a technical overview and a parts list — rather than a record of build progress.

What It Does

The microplotter is an in-house ultrasonic microdispensing system, proposed to print roughly 50 micron silver conductive traces onto two-dimensional structures. It is intended to be functionally equivalent to a commercial ultrasonic microplotter.

The design follows the ultrasonic pumping and sensing principles described in the published patent literature. An AC current applied to a piezoelectric element vibrates a tapered capillary near its resonance frequency; at low drive amplitude that motion pumps small, controlled amounts of ink through a fluid bridge at the substrate. The same piezoelectric response indicates when the meniscus or the tip loads against the surface, which is what makes controlled Z-height detection possible.

The proposal's stated targets are a feature width of about 50 microns on PDMS or SU-8 substrates, X–Y placement accuracy within 10 µm, bidirectional repeatability within 1 µm, and velocity ripple under 2 % at a traverse speed of 1 mm/s.

Subsystems

  • Gantry — the X and Y axes are ball-screw-driven linear stages, each a NEMA 17 stepper motor driving a ground ball screw through a bellows or Oldham coupling, with a magnetic angle encoder on the motor shaft for feedback.
  • Z axis — a micrometer driven by a stepper motor, converting rotation into precise linear motion.
  • Dispensing — a micropipette attached to a piezoelectric transducer, loaded by capillary action; vibration transferred along the pipette drives fluid out of the tip.
  • Standoff sensing — a current-sense resistor and envelope detector turn changes in piezo current into a slowly varying voltage, so that surface loading or the forming of the fluid bridge stops the descent.
  • Alignment — a vision system detects and centres on fiducial markers, which is what carries absolute X–Y positioning accuracy; the encoder loop handles stall detection and microstep linearization.
  • Software and firmware — a graphical interface converts vector and design files into gcode, and firmware interprets that gcode into synchronized X-Y-Z motion and piezoelectric drive commands, with tuning and diagnostics over a UART debug connection.
