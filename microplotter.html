Microplotter

Proposed

The project folder holds a written proposal — objectives and a technical design — rather than a record of build progress.

What It Does

The microplotter is an in-house ultrasonic microdispensing system for printing conductive traces roughly 50 microns wide, in silver colloid ink, onto flat substrates.

The proposal describes a three-axis Cartesian machine depositing onto PDMS or SU-8 substrates, targeting X–Y placement accuracy within 10 µm, bidirectional repeatability within 1 µm, and under 2% velocity ripple at a 1 mm/s traverse speed. It also sets out to establish a repeatable, documented process — parameters, calibration and quality control — that can be carried over to future structures.

Subsystems

  • Gantry — the X and Y axes are ball-screw-driven linear stages, each with a NEMA 17 stepper motor turning a ground ball screw through a backlash-tolerant coupling. A magnetic angle encoder on each motor shaft feeds position back to a PID loop, while absolute placement accuracy comes from a vision system centring on fiducial markers. The Z axis is a micrometer driven by a stepper motor.
  • Dispensing — a micropipette attached to a piezoelectric transducer. Vibration travels along the pipette and drives fluid out of the tip, dispensing small, controlled amounts. Capillary action loads the fixture; a continuous fluid supply is out of scope for this build.
  • Standoff sensing — the tip-to-substrate gap is found by piezo impedance. A current-sense resistor and envelope detector turn changes in piezo current into a slowly varying voltage, and a calibrated change in that signal during a slow descent indicates the tip has loaded against the surface, stopping the Z motion.
  • Software and firmware — a graphical interface converts vector and design files into G-code. Firmware interprets that G-code into synchronised X-Y-Z motion and piezoelectric drive commands, reads reference and fiducial markers to align the nozzle, and sets the dispensing height. A UART connection is used for tuning and diagnostics, with Ethernet or Bluetooth control as a stretch goal.
