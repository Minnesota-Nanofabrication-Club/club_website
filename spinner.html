Photoresist Spinner

In progress

The spinner's plan was rebaselined on 30 September 2026 around a minimal V1 motor-control bench prototype, with the previously integrated spinner design moved to V2. V1 is an ESP32-S3 development board, a brushless motor, a compatible electronic speed controller and a secured motor mount, controlled over USB serial; the first milestone is an unloaded motor demonstration — arm, start, timed run and stop under firmware control. Optical RPM measurement and, if open-loop control cannot hold the target, closed-loop speed regulation follow that demonstration.

What It Does

V2 is the integrated coating machine: 10 mm × 10 mm silicon chips about 525 µm thick, held on a bipolar electrostatic chuck and coated with AZ P4210 positive photoresist, with 4,000 RPM for 30 s as the starting coating trial. Optical RPM feedback and the same ESP32-S3 controller carry over from V1, inside a guarded enclosure with a hardwired safety chain independent of the firmware.

The spinner's proposed V1 acceptance target is 4,000 RPM within 2 %, measured over a continuous 30 s steady-state interval and repeated over five independent runs. Its own documentation is explicit that this is an initial engineering target rather than a figure derived from film-thickness data, and that it is to be revised against measured coating thickness and uniformity during the V2 process trials.
