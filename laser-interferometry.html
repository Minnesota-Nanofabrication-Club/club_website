Laser Interferometry

Architecture design

The folder holds a system overview and a proposed architecture rather than a record of assembly. Its timeline document is explicit that dates and the breakdown of the work are still being set, and lists what a working demonstration needs: the laser controller, the heterodyne laser, the software, a heater for the laser, and the optical setup.

What It Does

Because a stepper's projection field is smaller than the wafer, the substrate has to be stepped precisely. This metrology system is to provide real-time, high-speed closed-loop position feedback for the X–Y stage, eliminating stitching errors between exposure fields and allowing precise alignment during probing and testing.

Rather than a homodyne design, which is susceptible to DC noise, the proposed architecture is a heterodyne interferometer. Its light source is a Zeeman-split helium–neon laser: applying a magnetic field to a standard HeNe tube splits its output into two distinct optical frequencies. A beamsplitter sends one frequency to a fixed reference reflector and the other to a reflector that moves with the stage, so the moving arm returns frequency-shifted. The two beams recombine onto an optical receiver, and comparing the recombined beat frequency against the laser's own gives the displacement. An ESP32 handles the resulting beat frequencies, expected in the low megahertz, and passes the data to a PC.

Integration

  • Maskless stepper — the interferometer feeds position data straight to the motion controller, so that if the stage overshoots a move the software can digitally shift the pattern to align the exposure with the previous field.
  • Probe station — the same software can command sub-micron step-and-repeat moves between dies, so that the probes land squarely on the metal contacts without scratching the surrounding layers.
