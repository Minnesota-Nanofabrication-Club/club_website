Laser Interferometry

Architecture design

The folder holds a system overview and a parts list. The project's own timeline document says the dates are still being set, and lists what a working demonstration needs: a laser controller, the heterodyne laser, the optical setup, a heater for the laser, and software.

What It Does

A maskless stepper exposes a projection field smaller than the wafer, so the substrate has to be stepped precisely. This metrology system is intended to give the X/Y stage real-time, high-speed closed-loop position feedback, to eliminate stitching errors between fields and to allow precise alignment during probing and testing.

The proposed architecture is a heterodyne interferometer rather than a homodyne one, which is susceptible to DC noise. At its heart is a Zeeman-split helium-neon laser: applying a magnetic field to a standard HeNe tube splits its output into two distinct optical frequencies. A beam splitter sends one frequency to a reference reflector and the other to a reflector on the moving stage; the two return to the splitter and converge on an optical receiver, whose beat frequency differs from the source's by the motion of the measurement reflector. An ESP32 microcontroller handles the resulting low-megahertz signals and passes the data to a PC.

Where It Fits

  • Maskless stepper — the interferometer feeds position data directly to the motion controller, so that if the stage overshoots a move, the software can digitally shift the pattern to align the exposure with the previous field.
  • Probe station — the same software can command sub-micron step-and-repeat moves, so that test probes land squarely on the metal contacts without scratching the surrounding layers.
