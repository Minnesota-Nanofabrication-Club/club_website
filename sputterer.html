DC Magnetron Sputterer

In progress

Lead: Bear BlinSchauer

What It Does

Sputtering uses electric fields to accelerate material from a target onto a substrate. A process gas fills the chamber and is accelerated toward the target after losing an electron; the impact frees material from the target, and the freed material collects on the substrate. The target specification for this machine is to sputter aluminum metallization layers and bulk dielectric oxides.

Sputtering typically uses inert argon as the working gas. Introducing a reactive gas instead lets the freed material form a reaction product — an oxide or a nitride — on the substrate. Alternating the potential applied to the target, as an RF supply does, eliminates charge buildup and is what makes it possible to deposit insulating films such as aluminum oxide rather than metals alone.

Subsystems

The design is divided into five subsystems:

  • Magnetron and cooling
  • Vacuum
  • Process gas
  • Power supply
  • Chamber

Project Timeline

The build is planned in four phases across sixteen weeks, targeting a complete machine by winter break 2026.

PhaseTimelineWork
Procurement & planning Weeks 1 – 3
Aug 31 – Sep 20		 Finalize the mechanical CAD and the power-delivery and matching-network schematics; order the vacuum pumps, mass flow controllers, target materials and high-voltage components
Sub-systems & prep Weeks 4 – 6
Sep 21 – Oct 11		 Machine the custom structural components — magnetron housing, chamber mounts; bench-test the pumps and gauges as they arrive; build and wire the sub-systems in isolation, plumbing the gas control lines and wiring the power supply and matching network outside the chamber
Integration & prototyping (alpha) Weeks 7 – 10
Oct 12 – Nov 8		 Assemble the vacuum vessel, install the magnetron and connect the sub-systems to the chassis; pump down, leak-check and verify the ultimate base pressure; introduce a regulated argon flow at 1 – 50 mTorr and ignite and tune a stable plasma discharge on the cathode, with a buffer week for troubleshooting
Deposition & polishing (beta) Weeks 11 – 15
Nov 9 – Dec 13		 First real deposition — aluminum onto a glass slide — with film thickness and resistivity measured by four-point probe and power, pressure and distance tuned toward a 0.5 – 2.0 nm/s deposition rate; then reactive sputtering with oxygen for aluminum oxide or silicon dioxide; finally grounding and shielding enclosures, secured gas cylinders, and build documentation
