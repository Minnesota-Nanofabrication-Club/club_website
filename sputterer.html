DC Magnetron Sputterer

In progress

Lead: Bear Blinschauer

What It Does

Sputtering uses electric fields to accelerate material from a target onto a substrate. A process gas fills the chamber and is accelerated toward the target after losing an electron; the impact frees material from the target, and the freed material collects on the substrate. The target specification for this machine is to sputter aluminum metallization layers and bulk dielectric oxides.

Sputtering typically uses inert argon as the working gas. Introducing a reactive gas instead lets the freed material form a reaction product — an oxide or a nitride — on the substrate. Alternating the potential applied to the target, as an RF supply does, eliminates charge buildup and is what makes it possible to deposit insulating films such as aluminum oxide rather than metals alone.

Subsystems

The design is divided into five subsystems:

  • Magnetron and cooling
  • Vacuum
  • Process gas
  • Power supply
  • Chamber

Build Sequence

The build is planned as an ordered sequence of stages. The sequence itself carries no target dates.

  1. Complete the mechanical CAD design.
  2. Finalize the power delivery and matching network schematics.
  3. Procure components.
  4. Assemble the vacuum vessel and structural components.
  5. Pump down, leak-check, and verify the ultimate base pressure limits.
  6. Establish a dynamically regulated argon flow at a low process pressure, typically 1 – 50 mTorr.
  7. Ignite and tune a stable plasma discharge with the target material installed on the cathode.
  8. Test aluminum sputtering on a glass slide.
  9. Test aluminum or silicon with oxygen for dielectric deposition.
