About the Club

The Minnesota Nanofabrication Club (MNF) is a student organization at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities building an open, vertically integrated semiconductor program. The ability to scale computing systems is one of the defining engineering challenges of this decade: many of the fastest-growing technologies — AI, robotics, autonomous vehicles, satellites and quantum computing — depend directly on advances in computing hardware and software. To build the next generation of computers, we believe we should understand the entire computing stack, and our mission is to gain that understanding by building it ourselves.

Our initial goal is to fabricate micron-scale transistors while systematically improving our process toward submicron feature resolution. We intend to take part in the broader Hacker Fab community, and to publish our designs, measurements and project documentation where it is appropriate to do so.

Full Stack Codesign

Rather than take the computing stack as given, we are rebuilding it ourselves. The programme has two halves.

Build the Fab

Design, fabricate, and demonstrate a custom integrated circuit (IC) through a vertically integrated fabrication workflow, by the end of the 2026 fall semester.

Get Involved

Membership is open to all currently enrolled undergraduate students at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities. There are no prerequisites: we invite anyone who is hands-on, curious, passionate, and willing to work hard to join us.

The projects above span optics, mechanical design, vacuum systems, electronics, embedded control, process chemistry, and digital design — there is a subsystem for almost any engineering interest. Come to a meeting and claim one.

Questions, or want to get involved? Email Leonard Jin, club president, at jin00404@umn.edu.
