About the Club

The Minnesota Nanofabrication Club is a student organization at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities building an open, vertically integrated semiconductor program. Every abstraction exists for a reason — and to understand why each layer exists, we are rebuilding the entire stack ourselves from first principles: designing a custom compute kernel, developing the fabrication equipment and processes needed to manufacture it, and integrating both into a functioning silicon system.

Our initial goal is to fabricate micron-scale transistors while systematically improving our process toward submicron feature resolution. We are active in the broader Hacker Fab community and contribute open designs, documentation, and experimental results that make semiconductor fabrication more accessible.

Full Stack Codesign

Our program is one project with two halves: design, fabricate, and demonstrate a custom compute kernel through an open, vertically integrated semiconductor workflow.

Build the Fab

Develop an open semiconductor fabrication process — from fabrication equipment to functioning transistors and logic gates — capable of producing custom integrated circuits.

Design the Compute Kernel

Design a custom compute primitive that is mathematically sound, hardware efficient, and validated through simulation, FPGA implementation, and commercial silicon.

Current Projects

Each tool below is a self-contained build with its own architecture, timeline, and bill of materials. Together they make up the fabrication line.

  • Maskless Lithography Stepper In build — prototype targeted this semester

    Projects circuit patterns directly onto photoresist-coated substrates using a UV-retrofitted DLP projector, microscope optics, machine-vision alignment, and a motorized XYZ stage — no photomasks required.

  • Sputterer Design and bill of materials

    An RF sputtering chamber for depositing metallization layers and bulk dielectric oxides. RF drive lets us deposit insulating films such as aluminum oxide rather than metals alone. Subsystems in design: magnetron and cooling, vacuum, process gas, power supply, and chamber.

  • Tube Furnace Architecture design

    High-temperature furnace for the thermal steps of the process, including oxide growth and dopant drive-in.

  • Photoresist Spinner Architecture design

    Spin coater for depositing uniform photoresist films on substrates ahead of exposure.

  • Etcher Planned

    Etching system for transferring developed photoresist patterns into the underlying layers.

  • Compute Kernel In design

    The custom compute primitive the fab is being built to produce, validated through simulation, FPGA implementation, and commercial silicon before it reaches our own process.

Get Involved

Membership is open to all currently enrolled undergraduate students at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities. No prior fabrication experience is required — just curiosity about how chips are actually made.

The projects above span optics, mechanical design, vacuum systems, electronics, embedded control, process chemistry, and digital design — there is a subsystem for almost any engineering interest. Come to a meeting and claim one.
