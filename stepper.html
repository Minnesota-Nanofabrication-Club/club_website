Maskless Photolithography Stepper

The stepper is the first major system in the club's student-built semiconductor fabrication facility. It uses projected ultraviolet light and precision optics to transfer circuit patterns onto photoresist-coated substrates — no photomasks required. Patterns are defined digitally and projected directly, letting us iterate on designs as fast as we can draw them.

The design draws inspiration from publicly documented systems developed by Sam Zeloof, the Hacker Fab community, and Huygens Optics, as well as CMU's open-source stepper described in A low-cost, open-source maskless photolithography stepper for microfabrication.

How It Works

The system combines four subsystems that together control projection, focus, sample positioning, and pattern alignment:

  • Projection — a TI DLP projector, retrofitted with 410 nm UV LEDs, displays the circuit pattern.
  • Optics — a 10× microscope objective demagnifies the projected image onto the substrate, shrinking features to the micron scale.
  • Imaging — a machine-vision camera views the sample through the same optical path for focusing and pattern-to-pattern alignment.
  • Motion — a stepper-motor-driven XYZ stage positions the sample under the projection field, "stepping" across the wafer exposure by exposure.

Alignment accuracy and optical resolution are the two most important metrics of a stepper's performance, and both drive nearly every design decision in the system.
